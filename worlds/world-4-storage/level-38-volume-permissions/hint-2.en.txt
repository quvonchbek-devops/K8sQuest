🧭 Hint 2: Volume permissions

The problem: the container runs as a non-root user, but the volume is owned by
root. Result: no write permission.

Fix — add `fsGroup` to the pod spec:
```yaml
spec:
  securityContext:
    fsGroup: 1000    # changes the group owner of the volume files to this GID
  containers:
  - name: app
    securityContext:
      runAsUser: 1000    # the container runs as this UID
```

`fsGroup` adjusts the file permissions automatically when the volume is mounted.

See who is writing and whether fsGroup is set:

    kubectl get pod writer-app -n k8squest -o jsonpath='{.spec.securityContext}'
    kubectl get pod writer-app -n k8squest -o jsonpath='{.spec.containers[0].securityContext}'
    kubectl logs writer-app -n k8squest

The logs say `Permission denied`: the container runs as a non-root UID while the
volume belongs to another group — `fsGroup` closes that gap.

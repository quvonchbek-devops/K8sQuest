✅ Hint 3: Full solution

The pod uses `emptyDir` — it is born with the pod and DIES with the pod. For the
data to survive, the volume must be a PersistentVolumeClaim.

`spec.volumes` is an immutable field on a pod, so the pod is recreated. On top of
that the PVC ITSELF does not exist — it has to be created too.

1. Delete the old pod:

    kubectl delete pod data-app -n k8squest

2. Apply the PVC and the pod together (after `Show solution` the file is in your
   session):

    kubectl apply -f solution.yaml -n k8squest

   Or in the `Write your own manifest` panel — the key change is this:

```yaml
apiVersion: v1
kind: PersistentVolumeClaim
metadata:
  name: app-data
  namespace: k8squest
spec:
  accessModes: ["ReadWriteOnce"]
  resources:
    requests:
      storage: 1Gi
---
# inside the pod:
  volumes:
  - name: data
    persistentVolumeClaim:
      claimName: app-data     # INSTEAD OF emptyDir: {}
```

3. Check:

    kubectl get pvc app-data -n k8squest
    kubectl get pod data-app -n k8squest -o jsonpath='{.spec.volumes}'
    kubectl logs data-app -n k8squest

The PVC must be `Bound` and the volume must show `persistentVolumeClaim`.

4. NOW the most interesting step — see the persistence with your own eyes:

    kubectl delete pod data-app -n k8squest
    kubectl apply -f solution.yaml -n k8squest
    kubectl logs data-app -n k8squest

The log should show "Found existing data" and the earlier entries — with
emptyDir those entries would be gone.
